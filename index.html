The idea, in 30 seconds

  • Public by default is the problem. On a normal blockchain anyone can read balances, counterparties and history forever. That works for open markets and badly for real life.
  • Midnight splits the ledger. Midnight keeps a public state for what must be verifiable and a private state for what must stay yours, using zero-knowledge proofs — mostly generated on your own device.
  • Selective disclosure is the product. Prove “over 18”, “paid in full” or “this message is from me” without revealing the birth date, the amount, or the rest of the conversation.
  • NIGHT and DUST. NIGHT is Midnight’s token, held on Cardano; holding NIGHT generates DUST, the renewable resource that pays for shielded Midnight transactions.

Privacy tools — right here, nothing leaves your browser

1 · Paste-and-protect redactor

Paste a message, post or document before you share it. This finds email addresses, Cardano addresses and US phone numbers and masks them — locally, on this device only.

2 · Selective-disclosure planner

Tell it what a service is asking for. It sorts each item the Midnight way: share the raw data, share a proof instead, or keep it private entirely.

What are they asking for?

3 · NIGHT → DUST capacity estimator

Each NIGHT can sustain up to 5 DUST capacity — the model my NightDream Midnight desk uses. This is a capacity ceiling, not a promise: actual generation depends on holdings and Midnight network parameters.

4 · What does this dApp see?

Before you connect, tick what a dApp is asking to see or do. I sort each permission by exposure and explain what it really gives away, plus the Midnight alternative — a proof instead of the raw data. This checker never connects a wallet or signs anything.

What is the dApp asking for?

5 · Prove it, don’t show it — ZK claim simulator

Pick a claim, type the private value only you should know, and set the threshold. The simulator shows exactly what a verifier learns from a Midnight zero-knowledge proof — the claim, and whether it holds — and what stays hidden. It is a teaching simulation, not a cryptographic proof: the comparison runs locally on this device and your value never leaves this page.

6 · Compact snippet library — privacy patterns

Pick a pattern and see the Midnight split for it: what stays private on the user's device, what goes on the public ledger, and what is deliberately disclosed. These are simplified teaching patterns that show where each value belongs — they are not production contracts. Compact is still evolving: check the current docs and compile against the current release before using any pattern in a real contract.

7 · How DUST works — the lifecycle, step by step

DUST confuses everyone the first time: you do not buy it like a normal fee token. Holding NIGHT generates DUST up to a capacity ceiling, shielded transactions spend it, and holding the same NIGHT generates it back. Step through it with your own numbers. This is a teaching model of amounts and order only — it states no generation rate or time, because the real rate depends on Midnight network parameters.
